NutriTrack User Guide

Everything you need to track your daily nutrition, follow a personalised meal plan, and work with your dietitian — all in one place. No technical experience required.

Getting Started

Creating an Account

Anyone can register for a NutriTrack account. If your dietitian has already set up an account for you, skip to step 4.

  1. Open NutriTrack in your browser and click Register on the login page.
  2. Choose a username (this is what your friends and dietitian will see) and a password. You do not need an email address.
  3. Click Create Account. You will be logged in automatically.
  4. On your first visit you will land on the Dashboard. The app is ready to use straight away with default targets.
💡

No email needed. NutriTrack uses a username and password only. Write your password down — there is no password-reset feature yet.

Patient

Dashboard & Logging Food

The Dashboard is your home base. Every time you eat something, you log it here. The app adds up your nutrients throughout the day so you can see how close you are to your targets.

Understanding your macro cards

At the top of the Dashboard you will see four coloured cards — one for each macro and calories.

Protein
Builds and repairs muscle. Measured in grams (g). Most people need 100–200 g per day depending on body weight and goals.
Fat
Provides energy, supports hormones and absorbs vitamins. Measured in grams (g). Healthy fats come from nuts, oils, fish and avocado.
Carbohydrates
Your body's main fuel source. Measured in grams (g). Found in bread, rice, pasta, fruit and vegetables.
Calories
Total energy from everything you eat. Automatically calculated from your macros. Typical targets are 1,800–2,500 kcal per day.

Each card shows: consumed / target and how many grams remain. When a number turns red, you have gone over your target for the day.

Logging a food entry

  1. On the Dashboard, find the food entry form (below the macro cards).
  2. Start typing a food name in the search box — for example "chicken breast". A dropdown list will appear with matching foods.
  3. Click the food you want. The macro values fill in automatically.
  4. Enter the serving size (how many grams you ate) and select the unit (g, ml, piece, slice, or serving).
  5. Choose a meal type if you want (Breakfast, Lunch, Dinner, or Snack).
  6. Click Add. The food appears in your log below, and the macro cards update immediately.
✅

750+ foods included. NutriTrack has the full USDA nutrition database built in — search by name in English or Turkish.

Editing or deleting an entry

Find the entry in your log, click the pencil icon to edit the serving size or food name, or the trash icon to remove it entirely. Changes update your macro totals instantly.

Navigating dates

Use the ◀ and ▶ arrows next to today's date at the top of the Dashboard to view or log food for a different day. The Copy Yesterday button copies all of yesterday's entries to today — useful if you eat the same things regularly.

💡

Logging water. Below your macro cards you will find a water tracking widget. Tap the +200 ml, +250 ml, or +500 ml buttons after each glass or bottle you drink. Your daily water goal is set in Settings.

Patient

Setting Your Daily Targets

Your targets tell NutriTrack how much protein, fat, carbs and calories you are aiming for each day. The app ships with sensible defaults, but you should set them to match your personal goals.

  1. Click Settings in the top navigation bar.
  2. In the Macro Targets section, enter your daily goal for each macro in grams.
  3. The Calories field is optional — if you leave it blank the app calculates it automatically from your macros (protein × 4 + fat × 9 + carbs × 4).
  4. Click Save Targets.

Using the TDEE calculator

If you are not sure what your targets should be, use the built-in calculator. TDEE stands for Total Daily Energy Expenditure — the number of calories your body burns in a day.

  1. In Settings, scroll down to the TDEE Calculator.
  2. Enter your age, weight (kg), height (cm), and biological sex.
  3. Select your activity level — how active you are on a typical day, from sedentary (desk job, little exercise) to very active (hard training most days).
  4. Choose a goal: lose weight, maintain, or gain muscle.
  5. Click Calculate. Suggested macro targets appear below.
  6. Click Apply to Targets to save them.
⚠️

These are estimates. The TDEE calculator uses a general formula. If your dietitian has given you specific targets, enter those instead and ignore the calculator result.

Patient

Your Nutrition Plan

A nutrition plan is a structured weekly schedule that tells you what to eat at each meal, every day of the week. You can follow a ready-made starter template, or your dietitian may assign one directly to you.

Starting a plan from a template

  1. Click My Plan in the top navigation. (If you do not see this link, ask your dietitian to enable plan access for your account, or see the Admin section below.)
  2. If you have no plan yet, the page shows a Choose a Starter Plan screen with available 7-day templates.
  3. Read each template's name and description to find one that matches your goals.
  4. Click Start this plan. Your plan begins today.

Reading your plan

Once a plan is active, the My Plan page shows:

  • Plan name and description — an overview bar with how many days you are through the plan.
  • Today's meal slots — each slot represents a meal (Breakfast, Lunch, Dinner, Snack). Slots show suggested foods with quantities.
  • All 7 day tabs — click any day to preview its slots. Today's tab is highlighted.
  • Dietary Guidelines — rules from your dietitian, shown in a collapsible section at the bottom.
  • Weekly Category Progress — tracks how often you're eating from each food group (e.g. protein sources, dairy) against weekly targets.

Logging a meal slot

  1. Find the slot you have just eaten (e.g. Breakfast).
  2. Click the green Log button on that slot.
  3. A window opens showing Suggested foods for that slot. Click one to select it — the food and quantity fill in automatically.
  4. If you ate something different, use the Search food box to find any food in the database and set your own quantity.
  5. Click Log to save. The slot shows a green tick ✓ and the food is added to your daily log.
✅

Logging a slot also adds the food to your Dashboard. You will see the macros reflected in your daily totals straight away.

Optional slots

Some slots are marked optional. You do not have to log these — they might be an evening snack or a second coffee. Skip them freely.

Switching to a different plan

Click the Change Plan button in your plan overview to return to the template picker. Choosing a new template starts it from today and replaces your current plan.

Patient

My Foods

My Foods is your personal food library. It shows every food in the database and lets you add custom entries for foods that are not there — your favourite protein bar, a home-cooked recipe, or a local brand.

Searching the food database

  1. Click My Foods in the navigation.
  2. Type in the search box. Results appear from the 750+ USDA foods.
  3. Click a food card to see its full macro breakdown per 100 g.

Creating a custom food

  1. On the My Foods page, click + New Food.
  2. Enter the food name and its macros per 100 g (check the nutrition label on the packaging).
  3. Optionally set a default serving size and unit.
  4. Click Save. Your food appears in search results immediately.

Cloning a USDA food

USDA foods cannot be edited directly (they are read-only reference data). If the values are wrong for your specific brand, clone the food first:

  1. Find the USDA food and click Clone.
  2. A copy is created in your custom foods. Edit the name and macros to match your product's label.
  3. Save. From now on, use the cloned version when logging.
💡

All macros are per 100 g. When you log a food and enter a serving size (e.g. 150 g), NutriTrack scales the macros for you automatically.

Patient

Meal Templates

Meal templates are saved combos of foods you can log with a single tap. Perfect for meals you eat regularly — your usual breakfast, a post-workout shake, or a standard lunch.

Creating a template

  1. Click Meals in the navigation.
  2. Click + New Template.
  3. Give your template a name (e.g. Morning Oats) and a meal type.
  4. Click + Add Item, search for a food, and set the serving size. Repeat for each item in the meal.
  5. Click Save Template.

Using a template on the Dashboard

On the Dashboard, your saved templates appear as small chips just below the date navigation. Tap a chip to instantly log all its foods to the current day. A confirmation shows the total macros before you confirm.

✅

Templates log to today only. If you want to log a template to a past date, navigate to that date first using the date arrows, then tap the template chip.

Patient

Chat & AI Food Logging

The Chat page lets you describe what you ate in plain, everyday language — no need to search for each food separately. Type a sentence and the app figures out the foods and portions for you.

How to use it

  1. Click Chat in the navigation.
  2. Type what you ate, naturally. For example: "I had two scrambled eggs and a slice of whole wheat toast with butter" or "200g tavuk göğsü, bir kase pirinç".
  3. The app replies with the foods it identified and their macros.
  4. If the matches look right, confirm to add them to today's log.
  5. If a food is wrong, you can correct it by typing again.

Chat modes

NutriTrack uses different parsing methods depending on what is available:

  • Claude AI (best): If you or your dietitian has entered an Anthropic API key in Settings, NutriTrack uses Claude to understand your message. It handles complex sentences, regional foods, and brand names very well.
  • Offline NLP (good): Without an API key, NutriTrack uses a built-in language model to parse your message. Works for most common foods.
  • Simple matching (fallback): If the above fail, NutriTrack tries a basic word-match search.
💡

Getting an API key. Visit anthropic.com, create a free account, generate an API key, and paste it in Settings›Anthropic API Key. This enables the best chat experience. The key is stored only in your browser, never on the server.

Patient

History & Trends

The History page shows how your intake has changed over time — useful for spotting patterns, reviewing past days, and checking weekly averages.

  1. Click History in the navigation.
  2. The page loads your last 7 days by default. Use the date range picker to change the period.
  3. The calorie trend chart shows a bar for each day. A dashed line marks your calorie target — bars above the line are over-target days.
  4. Below the chart, each day has a summary card showing totals for protein, fat, carbs, and calories.
  5. Click any day's card to open that date in the Dashboard and see the full food log.
Patient

Social & Badges

NutriTrack has an optional social layer — connect with friends and family, compare weekly progress, and earn badges for consistent healthy habits.

Adding friends

  1. Click Friends in the navigation.
  2. Go to the Friends tab and enter your friend's username in the search box.
  3. Click Send Request. They will see a pending request on their Friends page.
  4. Once they accept, you are connected. You can see each other's daily summaries in the Feed, and compete in the weekly Race.

Feed & privacy

The Feed tab shows a timeline of your friends' daily nutrition summaries. Each person controls exactly what they share — they can choose to show or hide calories and specific macros from their feed. Go to Friends›Feed›Privacy Settings to control your own visibility.

Weekly Race (leaderboard)

The Race tab shows a leaderboard of you and your friends, ranked by weekly game score. Scores reset every Monday. The scoring system:

ActionPoints
Each macro within 90–110% of target+25 pts each
Water goal reached for the day+5 pts
Daily note written+3 pts
First food logged before 9 am+5 pts (early bird)
Maximum daily score115 pts

Badges

Badges are earned automatically and shown on the Badges tab:

  • 7-Day Streak — log food 7 days in a row
  • Perfect Week — hit all macro targets every day for a week
  • Protein King — consistently hit your protein target
  • Hydration Hero — hit your water goal every day for a week
  • Early Bird — log before 9 am for 5 consecutive days
  • Consistent 30 — log food for 30 days in a row
Patient

Settings

Settings is where you adjust your personal targets, calculator preferences, and optional integrations.

Language

Click the 🌐 EN / TR button in the top navigation to switch the interface language between English and Turkish. Food names also switch language where translations are available.

Macro Targets

Enter daily goals for protein, fat, carbs, and calories. Click Save Targets. Your history is preserved — changing targets does not alter past records.

Water goal

Set your daily water target in ml (a common target is 2,000–3,000 ml). This controls the progress bar in the water widget on the Dashboard.

Anthropic API Key

Paste your personal Anthropic API key here to enable Claude-powered Chat. The key is saved in your browser only — it is never sent to or stored on the server. You can clear it at any time.

Dietitian

Admin Access

Dietitian features are available to accounts with admin status. When you log in as an admin, you will see extra items in the navigation: Dietitian, Recipes, Exchanges, and Admin.

⚠️

Admin accounts are set up by the system administrator. If you are a dietitian who needs admin access, contact the person who manages the NutriTrack installation for your practice.

What admins can do

  • View and manage all user accounts
  • Enable the Plan feature for specific patients
  • Create structured 7-day nutrition plans
  • Assign plans to patients with a custom start date
  • Create food exchange categories and recipes
  • Access patient fulfilment records (with patient consent)

Notification bell

Admin accounts see a bell 🔔 icon in the top navigation. It shows a count of patients who have recently granted you access to view their data. Click the bell to see the full list.

Managing Patients

The Admin panel is where you view all registered users, enable features for them, and manage their accounts.

  1. Click Admin in the navigation.
  2. The Users list shows every account on the system — username, registration date, and whether the plan feature is enabled.
  3. Find the patient you want to work with.
  4. Toggle Plan Feature Enabled on. This makes the My Plan menu item appear for that patient.
  5. To assign a plan, click Assign Plan next to the patient's name — see the Assigning Plans section below.
✅

Patients can also self-assign starter templates from the My Plan page without dietitian involvement. Use admin assignment when you want to provide a personalised plan.

Dietitian

Creating Nutrition Plans

A plan is built from the top down: a Plan contains Days, each day contains Meal Slots, and each slot contains Items — the specific foods or food choices for that meal.

Creating a new plan

  1. Click Dietitian in the navigation.
  2. Click + New Plan.
  3. Enter a plan name, optional description, and duration (typically 7 days).
  4. Choose whether this plan is a Template (visible to all users for self-assignment) or a private plan you will assign to specific patients only.
  5. Click Create.

Adding days and slots

  1. Open the plan you just created. You will see a list of days (Day 1 through Day 7).
  2. Click + Add Slot on a day to add a meal slot (e.g. Breakfast, Lunch, Afternoon Snack).
  3. Name the slot and choose a Content Pattern (see below).
  4. Optionally mark the slot as Optional — the patient can skip it without it affecting their progress score.
  5. Add items to the slot (the foods or food choices for that meal).
  6. Repeat for each day of the plan. Days can share the same structure or vary.

Content Patterns explained

Each slot has a content pattern that defines how rigid or flexible the food choice is:

PatternNameWhat it meansExample
A Fixed The exact food is specified. The patient should eat this food. Grilled chicken breast, 130 g
B Exchange The patient picks one food from a defined category (e.g. Protein Sources). Rotation rules can limit how often each food repeats. Any protein source — chicken, beef, fish, or lentils
F Conditional The slot only applies on certain conditions (e.g. training days, or if the patient is still hungry). Often combined with is_optional. Post-workout protein shake — only on gym days
💡

Two built-in templates ship with NutriTrack: Balanced Weekly Plan A — Protein Rotation (Pattern B dinners rotating through 7 proteins) and Balanced Weekly Plan B — Fixed Main & Salad Rotation (fixed dinner with a rotating salad swap). Study these for examples of all patterns in practice.

Dietitian

Exchange Categories

Exchange categories group foods that are nutritionally interchangeable — for example, all protein sources a patient can choose from on a given day. They power the Pattern B (Exchange) slots in your plans.

Creating a category

  1. Click Exchanges in the navigation.
  2. Click + New Category.
  3. Name the category (e.g. Lean Protein Sources) and add an optional description.
  4. Add members — the foods that belong to this category. Search the food database and add them one by one.
  5. Save the category.

Using categories in a plan

When you add a Pattern B item to a slot, link it to an exchange category. The patient will then see all the foods in that category as options when logging the slot. You can also set a rotation frequency — the maximum number of times per week a given food from the category should appear, encouraging dietary variety.

✅

Built-in categories (from starter templates): Lean Protein Sources, Dairy & Dessert, Main Dish Salads, and Balanced Lunch Set are pre-seeded and available to use in your plans immediately.

Dietitian

Assigning Plans to Patients

Once you have built a plan, assign it to a patient from the Admin panel. Only one plan can be active per patient at a time — assigning a new plan automatically deactivates the previous one.

  1. Click Admin in the navigation and find the patient.
  2. Click Assign Plan next to their username.
  3. A window opens. Select the plan from the dropdown list.
  4. Set the start date — this is Day 1 of the plan for the patient. You can backdate it if the patient already started following the plan on paper.
  5. Click Assign. The patient now sees the plan on their My Plan page.
💡

The start date matters. NutriTrack calculates which day of the plan the patient is on by counting days from the start date. If today is 5 days after the start date, the patient is on Day 6.

Dietitian

Monitoring Patient Progress

You can view how well a patient is following their assigned plan — which slots they have logged, which foods they chose, and how their weekly category quotas are tracking. Patient consent is required.

Requesting data access

Patients control whether you can see their data. When you assign a plan to a patient, a consent toggle appears on their Settings page. Until they enable it, their data remains private.

  1. Ask the patient to open Settings and look for the Dietitian Access section.
  2. They click Allow access next to your name.
  3. You will receive a notification in the bell 🔔 menu confirming access has been granted.
  4. From the Dietitian panel, click the patient's name to open their progress view.

What you can see

  • Slot fulfilment records — which slots were logged each day, and which food they chose.
  • Weekly Category Progress — how many portions from each exchange category were consumed vs the quota you set in the plan.
  • Daily notes — any notes the patient wrote (hunger, energy levels, how they felt).
⚠️

Macro totals are not shown in the dietitian view by default. Patients control whether their calorie and macro data is visible to you through their privacy settings in the Feed section.

Recipes

Click Recipes in the navigation to manage the recipe library. Recipes work like multi-ingredient foods — define the ingredients, and NutriTrack calculates the total macros per serving. Recipes can be assigned as items in plan slots, and patients see the recipe name when they log the slot.
